California Med Spa Policy and Procedure Manual

State-Specific Policies for Aesthetic Nurses, NPs, PAs and Practice Owners · Cindi Vokey, BSN, RN, LNC

Sources Reviewed September 2026

California aesthetic practices run on standardized procedures, physician supervision and a clear line between the physician's examination and what can be delegated to a registered nurse. Your California manual is built around those rules.

Who Regulates Aesthetic Practice in California

  • California Board of Registered Nursing
  • Medical Board of California
  • Cal/OSHA (Bloodborne Pathogens State Plan)
  • California Department of Public Health, Medical Waste Management Program

Key California Rules Your Manual Addresses

Standardized Procedures and the Prior Examination

The Medical Board of California states that an appropriate prior examination is required before a prescriptive drug or device procedure, that the examination may not be delegated to a registered nurse, and that a physician may then delegate the procedure to an RN acting under standardized procedures. The same guidance states that an NP or PA may be delegated the prior examination (Medical Board of California, The Business of Medicine: Medical Spas).

Physician Supervision and Availability

The Medical Board's medical spa guidance states that the supervising physician need not be onsite but must be immediately reachable, and that paper-only supervision and rent-a-license arrangements are not acceptable (Medical Board of California, Medical Spas).

Lasers and IPL

The Medical Board's cosmetic treatment FAQs state that RNs and PAs may perform laser and IPL hair, spider-vein and tattoo removal under physician supervision, and that LVNs, medical assistants, cosmetologists, electrologists and estheticians may not (Medical Board of California, Cosmetic Treatments FAQs).

Procedures the Official Sources Don't Address Directly

Dermal fillers, Kybella, PRP, sclerotherapy, PDO threads and IV hydration are not addressed procedure by procedure in the Medical Board guidance reviewed for this page. Your manual flags these items for confirmation with the Board of Registered Nursing and your medical director before you offer them.
