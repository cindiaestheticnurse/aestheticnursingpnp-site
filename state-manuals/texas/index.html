Texas Med Spa Policy and Procedure Manual

State-Specific Policies for Aesthetic Nurses, NPs, PAs and Practice Owners · Cindi Vokey, BSN, RN, LNC

Texas aesthetic practice depends on physician delegation and written delegation orders. Separate rules apply to laser hair removal facilities, and a 2025 statute covers elective IV therapy. Your Texas manual brings these rules together in one place.

Who Regulates Aesthetic Practice in Texas

  • Texas Board of Nursing
  • Texas Medical Board
  • Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (Laser Hair Removal)
  • Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (Medical Waste)

Key Texas Rules Your Manual Addresses

Physician Delegation for Cosmetic Injectables

The Texas Board of Nursing states that cosmetic medications such as Botox and Restylane require physician delegation, and that RNs practice under their own licenses and may not diagnose or prescribe (Texas Board of Nursing, April 2019 Bulletin).

Elective IV Therapy

Under Texas Occupations Code Chapter 172, effective September 1, 2025, a physician may delegate ordering of elective IV therapy to a PA or APRN, and administration to a PA, APRN or RN, each acting under adequate physician supervision (Texas Occupations Code Chapter 172; Texas Medical Board, Who Can Do IV Hydration?).

Lasers and Laser Hair Removal

Texas Board of Nursing Position Statement 15.9 states that delivering laser energy is not within nursing scope as an independent nursing function (Texas Board of Nursing Position Statements). Laser hair removal facilities must also be registered with TDLR and have a consulting physician contract (TDLR Laser Hair Removal FAQ).

Procedures the Official Sources Don't Address Directly

Kybella, PRP, sclerotherapy and PDO threads are not specifically addressed in the Board of Nursing materials reviewed for this page. Your manual flags them for procedure-specific confirmation before you offer them.
