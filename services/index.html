Buyer's Evaluation Checklist

How to Evaluate Any Policy and Procedure Manual.

In a 2024 California Board of Registered Nursing Case, an RN's Purchased Association Templates Were Described as "generic templates that did not have the name of the facility, date of creation, review, or revision, or signatures of the medical director or any other staff." Here Are the Questions We Recommend Asking Before You Buy Any Manual.

What to CheckQuestions to Ask Any Provider, Including Us
State-Specific EditionDoes It Address Your State's Board of Nursing and Medical Board Rules on Scope, Delegation and Supervision, and Say Which State It Was Written For?
Your Practice on Every PageAre Your Practice Name, Medical Director and Providers Built In, or Left for You to Adapt?
Standardized Procedure ElementsDoes Each Standardized Procedure Address Every Element Your State Requires, Such as Those Listed in California's 16 CCR 1474?
Traceable SourcesCan You See the FDA Labeling and Peer-Reviewed Sources Behind Each Instruction, and When Each Document Was Last Reviewed?
Product-Specific DosingAre Dosing, Dilution and Maximum Volumes Stated per Product and Injection Site?
Complication ManagementAre Recognition, Escalation and Treatment Steps Written Out, Including Vascular Occlusion?
Approvals and Version HistoryIs There a Place for Approval Signatures, Annual Review Dates and Revision History on Each Policy?
Provider Authorization and CompetencyDoes It Record Who May Perform Each Procedure, How Competency Is Validated and How Often?
Connected DocumentationAre Consents, Charting Templates, Patient Instructions and Competency Records Available for Each Treatment?
Implementation SupportIs Rollout Help Available So Your Medical Director and Team Can Approve, Train On and Use It?

Products Vary and Change, so Compare Any Manual Against Your Own State's Requirements and Ask to See a Redacted Sample. Standardized Procedure Elements: California Code of Regulations, Title 16, Section 1474.
