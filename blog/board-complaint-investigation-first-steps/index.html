Board Complaint or Investigation: First Steps for Aesthetic Nurses

October 29, 2026 · Cindi Vokey, BSN, RN

Most of what's online about Board complaints tells patients how to file one. Very little tells the aesthetic nurse what to do when the letter arrives. Here's how the process works in California, and the first steps to take.

How the California BRN Process Works

According to the California Board of Registered Nursing:

  1. Review. The BRN reviews each complaint to decide whether it has authority to investigate. It can only investigate possible violations of the Nursing Practice Act or Board regulations.
  2. Investigation. Complaints within its authority are investigated by the Department of Consumer Affairs Division of Investigation and/or Board nursing consultants. The most serious allegations get priority.
  3. Closure or action. If no violation can be substantiated, the case is closed. If evidence shows a violation that warrants discipline, the case is resolved through informal or formal proceedings.
  4. Accusation. An Accusation is the legal document that formally charges a registered nurse with violating the Nursing Practice Act. It also notifies the public that disciplinary action is pending.

The BRN says the process can take an extended time depending on complexity, and that information is confidential during the investigation.

First Steps When You Receive Notice

  1. Don't ignore deadlines. Note every date on the letter.
  2. Talk to a nursing-license defense attorney before you respond. Don't send a statement first.
  3. Gather, don't alter. Collect your standardized procedures, GFE records, consent forms, training records and supervision documentation. Never change existing records.
  4. Check your supervision paper trail. In aesthetic cases, the questions often go beyond the treatment itself to who examined, who ordered, and who supervised.
  5. Keep it confidential. Don't discuss the case on social media or with colleagues.
