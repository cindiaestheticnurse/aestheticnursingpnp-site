Med Spa Compliance Questions, Answered by an Aesthetic Nurse

What Is Med Spa Compliance?

It's following the state and federal rules that apply to a practice offering medical aesthetic treatments, including ownership, medical director oversight, the good faith exam, scope and delegation, product sourcing, safety, privacy and advertising, and documenting how your practice meets each one.

Read the Med Spa Compliance Guide →

What Should Medical Director Oversight Look Like?

Regulators are looking for real clinical oversight, not just a signature on a contract. Document a written agreement that matches your state's rules, how the medical director is reached during treatment hours, chart review and site visit logs, and backup coverage.

See the Medical Director Agreement Checklist →

How Do Aesthetic Nurses Stay Within Scope?

Your policies should say which roles may perform each treatment, under what supervision and with what training. How that's written depends on your state: standing delegation orders, protocols, collaborative agreements or, in California, standardized procedures.

Compare State-Specific Manuals →

Who Is Responsible for Compliance in a Med Spa?

Responsibility is shared. Owners are responsible for the business structure and operations, the medical director for clinical oversight and delegation, and each licensed provider for practicing within their own scope. Your policies should name who is responsible for each area.

See What 35 Board Accusations Show →

Does Buying a Policy Template Make My Med Spa Compliant?

No. A template can be a starting point, but it has to be customized to your state, your treatments and your providers, then approved, signed and followed. No template or manual guarantees a regulatory outcome.

How Our Manuals Are Customized →

Do Med Spa Rules Differ by State?

Yes, significantly. Ownership, supervision, the good faith exam, delegation and registration requirements all vary by state, and several states have passed med spa-specific laws since 2025.
